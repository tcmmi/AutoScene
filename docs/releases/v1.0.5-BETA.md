## What's new

- **Much better recognition in anime.** AutoScene now tells anime characters apart far more reliably. In our tests, most of a main character's scenes used to end up on someone else; now about nine in ten land on the right character.
- **A Best choice for anime.** **Settings → General → Anime characters** offers Automatic, Standard and Best. Best gets even more of each character's scenes right, after a one-time download of about 1.2 GB, and analysis takes about a fifth longer. Automatic picks Best when your graphics card is built for AI work, such as an RTX card.
- **Rescan: a second look at faces, in every kind of footage.** Select faces or characters and choose **Rescan** (or press R). AutoScene reads them again at full quality from your original file and suggests who each one is. It also asks when two characters might be one person. In our 3D animation test it gave a clear answer for nearly three times as many hard faces, and found who 25 unclaimed faces belong to instead of 4. Nothing changes until you say so.
- **Remove black bars.** A new switch in the export and scenepack dialogs cuts off the bars built into the picture.
- **Match loudness.** Another switch brings every clip to the same volume. Both switches are remembered for next time.
- **Safer exports.** An export that won't fit on its drive is stopped before it starts, and your computer stays awake while it exports. If your graphics card fails on a clip, AutoScene makes that clip another way instead of giving up. Bright, HDR footage turned into normal video no longer shows bands in smooth skies and shadows.

## Before you install

- **Already installed?** The app updates itself and keeps your projects and models. Anime projects you made before then find their characters again by themselves, with the better recognition, after a download of about 380 MB (plus about 1.2 GB where Best is picked). Your names and decisions stay.
- **No going back.** Once 1.0.5 has opened a project, 1.0.4 can't open it any more.
- **Rescan is new.** It doesn't work with the Best anime model yet, and we've tested it most on animated footage, so please tell us how it does on yours.
- **Not tried on a real Mac yet.** We tested on virtual Macs, so speed, heat and some features may differ on yours. Linux hasn't run on a real desktop either, so please tell us what you see.
- **Not signed by a publisher yet,** so the first start needs your OK. On Windows, choose **More info → Run anyway**. On macOS, drag AutoScene to Applications and open it, then choose **Open Anyway** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and confirm. With Smart App Control on, Windows 11 offers no such choice and blocks the installer. If macOS later refuses a part of the app, the fix is in the full list at the very end.

## Check your download

Each file comes with a signed record (a build attestation) of the workflow
of [tcmmi/AutoScene](https://github.com/tcmmi/AutoScene) that built it, and
the tag and commit it was built from. To check one, use the
[GitHub CLI](https://cli.github.com), signed in with any GitHub account:

```bash
gh attestation verify AutoScene_1.0.5-BETA_x64-setup.exe -R tcmmi/AutoScene
```

To insist on this tag and the release workflow, add
`--source-ref refs/tags/v1.0.5-BETA --signer-workflow tcmmi/AutoScene/.github/workflows/release.yml`.

Or compare SHA-256 hashes: put `SHA256SUMS` next to your downloads and run
`sha256sum --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS` (Linux) or
`shasum -a 256 --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS` (macOS). In PowerShell
on Windows, this prints `True` for an intact download:

```powershell
(Get-FileHash .\AutoScene_1.0.5-BETA_x64-setup.exe).Hash -eq (-split (Select-String -Path SHA256SUMS -Pattern x64-setup.exe -SimpleMatch).Line)[0]
```

### Anime

- **Better recognition.** Anime projects find their characters with a sharper model. On two hand-checked anime episodes, the faces of the main cast landed on their own character about nine times in ten, where before it was fewer than half. The anime models are now a download of about 590 MB in all, and the anime analysis takes about a third longer.
- **Best.** **Settings → General → Anime characters** chooses Automatic, Standard or Best. Best adds a second model that looks at each character's clearest faces: on the same episodes, it put about 94 % of the main characters' screen time on the right character, against about 81 % with Standard. It's a one-time download of about 1.2 GB, and analysis takes about a fifth longer. Automatic picks Best when your graphics card is built for AI work, such as an RTX card, and the row says what it picked here. Fast Scan keeps the Standard model.
- **Switching models.** When the choice comes to another model, anime projects find their characters again in the background. Your names and decisions stay.
- **3D animation and live action** keep the models they had: the ones we tried did no better there.

### Rescan

- **A second look.** Select faces in a character's grid, or characters in the Cast, and choose **Rescan** (key R). AutoScene reads those faces again from your original file, at its full quality, and compares each with every character.
- **What Rescan found.** A page shows its suggestions: faces that look like someone, faces that could be either of two characters, faces that might not be who they're with, and things that might not be faces at all. A tick keeps a face with the character shown, a cross says no.
- **Same person?** When two characters might be one person, Rescan asks, with their clearest faces side by side and whether they were ever in the same frame. You answer **Same person** or **Two people**.
- **Your call.** Rescan changes nothing by itself. Only your answers move faces, and they work like **Move to…**, so other unconfirmed faces may follow.
- **How much it helps.** On a 3D animated series, Rescan gave a clear answer for 26 hard faces where the first pass had 9, and recognised 25 faces in **Everyone else** instead of 4. Hard faces are also read in frames the first pass skipped. Faces that were already clear stay as they are, since reading them again gained nothing.
- **Limits.** Rescan needs the original file. It doesn't work with the Best anime model yet. We haven't measured it on live action yet, so please tell us how it does there.

### Exports

- **Remove black bars.** A switch in the export and scenepack dialogs. AutoScene looks at 20 frames of each episode once, and cuts off the bars they all agree on, so a dark scene isn't mistaken for a bar. In one merged file, an episode with other bars is padded back to the same size. The size estimate doesn't count the crop yet, so it reads a little high.
- **Match loudness.** A switch that brings every clip to the same volume (-16 LUFS), without letting it clip. Very short clips take their episode's level. Both switches are remembered with the format. Bit-Perfect does neither, because it stays the source.
- **Room on the drive.** An export or pack that can't fit on its drive is refused before it starts, and a full disk says so.
- **Stays awake.** Your computer doesn't go to sleep during exports, indexing or previews.
- **Hardware encoder trouble.** A clip your graphics card's encoder fails on is made in software. A merged export starts over in software when its failing part comes out that way. AMD and Intel encoders lose options they don't support, and cards that can't do 10-bit HEVC are found at the check.
- **Cleaner pictures.** HDR footage turned into normal video is dithered, so gradients don't band. Normal video carries its colour information on the frames too, so players show it right.
- **Better sound size.** The audio bitrate follows the number of channels, in the size estimate too.
- **Fewer failures.** Sources tagged as RGB colour no longer fail to export.
- **Easier help.** The log now records every FFmpeg command and how each export was set up, so a failed export is easier to sort out when you send us the log.

### Installing and updating

- **Once after the update.** Anime projects you made before find their characters again, one episode after another, after downloading the new model (about 380 MB, plus about 1.2 GB where Best is picked). Your names and decisions stay.
- **Going back.** Once 1.0.5 has opened a project, 1.0.4 can't open it.
- **Not tried on a real Mac yet.** This version was built and tested on GitHub's virtual Macs, which have no Neural Engine or GPU, so a real Mac may behave differently. Linux has run in CI and WSL only, not on a real desktop. If you try either, please tell us what you see.
- **First start on macOS, step by step.** Drag AutoScene to Applications, open it and close the message that macOS can't verify it. Then open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down to the note about AutoScene, choose **Open Anyway** and confirm. (Since macOS 15, right-click → Open no longer does this.) If macOS later refuses a part of the app, such as `libonnxruntime.dylib`, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/AutoScene.app` once.
