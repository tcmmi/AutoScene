AutoScene 1.0.4 Beta is a big one: plugins are here, ready to add new things to the app from a catalog we review. Anime projects now find their characters, animated casts can get name suggestions you approve, and your Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve keys work here too. The player gives the picture more room, and the scenepack builder got calmer.

## What's new

- **Plugins are here.** They can add new things to AutoScene, from export actions and scenepack filters to whole pages of their own. Open **Plugins → Browse** for the first two, and you'll see what a plugin asks for before it installs.
- **A Plugins area on our Discord.** There's a page for every plugin in ・plugins, a handbook in ・plugin-docs, ・submit-plugin to send your own for review and ・plugin-updates for what's new. Join at https://discord.gg/autoscene.
- **AutoScene finds characters in anime now.** Anime projects used to stop at their shots, and now they find their characters too. Fast Scan works on anime as well: quicker, at some cost in accuracy.
- **Name suggestions for animated casts.** Turn on **Character names from AniList, TVmaze and Fandom** in **Settings → General**, and AutoScene looks up names for anime, animated series and films. A banner offers them, and you accept them with one click or leave some out.
- **Use your Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve keys.** In **Settings → Shortcuts**, start from their keys or AutoScene's. Then change nearly any key to your own.
- **Take, drop or invert a whole scenepack at once.** Each is one shortcut, with Undo. All the filters now sit in one tidy menu, and Export shows how far along it is.
- **A player that puts the picture first.** The picture fills the stage, even from a small preview copy. The details share one panel with **Cast** and **File** tabs, and In, Out and Export sit still under the picture.
- **EDLs, cut lists and easier clip names.** An export's menu can save an EDL or a cut list, so you can rebuild the cut from the episodes. Buttons now add the series, character, episode and more to your clip names.
- **Fewer false shots, and stacks.** In newly analysed episodes, a camera move, smoke or a change of light rarely splits a shot now, though some real transitions such as wipes get missed. In a character's Shots view, shots that follow each other closely stack on one card.
- **Fast Scan follows your computer.** New projects start with it where a full scan is heavy work, such as built-in graphics, older cards, Macs and Linux: it's quicker but finds less. **Settings → General** changes that, and on Windows and Macs it can also choose what analysis runs on.
- **AutoScene speaks Romanian.** Choose Română in **Settings → General → App language**, or let AutoScene follow your computer's language. It's a first draft, so corrections are very welcome.
- **And plenty of polish.** Big casts are easier to search and sort, progress bars are calmer, and Undo and error messages now show in the installed app. Discord can show you're working in AutoScene if you turn that on, though we haven't tried it with the real Discord app yet.

## Before you install

- **Already installed?** The app updates itself and keeps your projects and models. Anime projects you made before then start finding their characters by themselves, after a download of about 360 MB. Character pictures are picked again once, and ones you chose stay.
- **What goes online.** AutoScene asks GitHub for the plugin list at each start and once a day, so GitHub sees your IP address. A switch in **Plugins → Browse** turns that off, but then you won't hear about withdrawn plugins. Character names, if you turn them on, send the project's title or your search to the name sites, which see your IP address too. So do the sites behind NVIDIA's optional download and the Contributors page.
- **Plugins run on your computer.** A plugin that is a program can do anything you can, whatever it says it needs. Both plugins in the catalog today are programs, and a person read them first. Plugins we haven't reviewed stay blocked until you allow them.
- **Not tried on a real Mac yet.** We tested on virtual Macs, so speed, heat and some new features may differ on yours. Linux hasn't run on a real desktop either, so please tell us what you see.
- **Not signed by a publisher yet,** so the first start needs your OK. On Windows, choose **More info → Run anyway**. On macOS, drag AutoScene to Applications and open it, then choose **Open Anyway** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and confirm. With Smart App Control on, Windows 11 offers no such choice and blocks the installer. If macOS later refuses a part of the app, the fix is in the full list at the very end.

## Check your download

Each file comes with a signed record (a build attestation) of the workflow
of [tcmmi/AutoScene](https://github.com/tcmmi/AutoScene) that built it, and
the tag and commit it was built from. To check one, use the
[GitHub CLI](https://cli.github.com), signed in with any GitHub account:

```bash
gh attestation verify AutoScene_1.0.4-BETA_x64-setup.exe -R tcmmi/AutoScene
```

To insist on this tag and the release workflow, add
`--source-ref refs/tags/v1.0.4-BETA --signer-workflow tcmmi/AutoScene/.github/workflows/release.yml`.

Or compare SHA-256 hashes: put `SHA256SUMS` next to your downloads and run
`sha256sum --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS` (Linux) or
`shasum -a 256 --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS` (macOS). In PowerShell
on Windows, this prints `True` for an intact download:

```powershell
(Get-FileHash .\AutoScene_1.0.4-BETA_x64-setup.exe).Hash -eq (-split (Select-String -Path SHA256SUMS -Pattern x64-setup.exe -SimpleMatch).Line)[0]
```

### Plugins

- **Plugins, and a catalog to find them in.** A plugin adds to AutoScene: an entry in each export's menu, a command in the palette, a filter in a character's scenepack, a way to save a pack as a timeline file, something that runs by itself after an analysis or an export (off until you turn it on), or whole pages of its own. **Plugins** has its own tile in the rail. Each run is a job in Activity with a Stop button. The design started from moongetsu's prototype.
- **Browse** lists the plugins of AutoScene's catalog, which is live at [tcmmi/autoscene-plugins](https://github.com/tcmmi/autoscene-plugins). Its first two are AutoScene's own examples: Hello Export, which writes an export's clip list beside it, and Discord Notify, which posts a finished export's clip list, or the end of an analysis, to a Discord channel through your own webhook.
- **The catalog check.** AutoScene asks GitHub for the catalog at the start and once a day, for updates and withdrawn plugins, and GitHub sees your IP address. A switch under Plugins turns that off, and then AutoScene doesn't learn of withdrawn plugins. You start every update yourself, and one that asks for anything new opens its review.
- **Reviewed by AutoScene** means the catalog's CI built that version from its public source and a person read it. A version AutoScene withdraws is turned off and doesn't run. A plugin from anywhere else says "Says it is by …", and runs only once you turn on **Allow plugins that AutoScene has not reviewed**. That switch and each such install ask in a dialog of your system's own, and you type the plugin's name in its review.
- **Before anything installs**, a review shows what the plugin asks for, what it downloads, from where and how much. You can also drop an `.asp` file on the window, or open it from your file manager (not from the AppImage, which registers nothing there). On Windows, the first double-click may ask which app to use: choose AutoScene.
- **The sandbox.** WebAssembly plugins carry the **Sandboxed** badge: they see only their own folder and what their permissions give them, and AutoScene enforces that. What they read, they can still send to the hosts they name. A plugin's pages run in a sandboxed frame too, though on Windows and macOS a page can send a little out through WebRTC. A plugin that is a program is different: it runs with your user rights, and its permissions are a promise shown before you install it, not a wall. Both plugins in the catalog today are programs.
- **Writing one.** The [plugin guide](https://github.com/tcmmi/AutoScene/blob/main/docs/PLUGINS.md) has a quick start near the top, `asp init` makes a plugin to begin from, and [seven examples](https://github.com/tcmmi/AutoScene/tree/main/examples/plugins) cover Python, Rust, pages and services. `asp` isn't in the downloads: you build it from a checkout of the repository, with Rust, and lock scripts with uv 0.13.7 or older. Python plugins that run as programs use AutoScene's own uv, which AutoScene gets once, at the first run (about 15 MB, plus a Python if the computer has none), so nobody installs anything by hand. That first run needs the internet. To get into the catalog, open a pull request that names your public source and its commit.
- **On our Discord.** The new Plugins area has a page for every plugin in ・plugins, a handbook in ・plugin-docs, ・submit-plugin to send your own for review and ・plugin-updates for what's new. Join at https://discord.gg/autoscene.

### Anime and animation

- **Anime characters.** Anime projects used to stop at their shots. They now find their characters, by their heads and whole look, with models made for anime.
- **Names.** With **Character names from AniList, TVmaze and Fandom** on (**Settings → General**, off by default), AutoScene looks the names up by itself once the characters are found, when the project's title clearly names the series or film (otherwise you search by hand): an anime's on AniList, an animated series' on TVmaze, an animated film's on its franchise's Fandom wiki. A banner offers them, and you give them with one click or leave some out, because a name can be wrong. Only the project's title or your search text goes out, then requests for the matching cast and pictures; AniList, TVmaze or, for films, Wikidata and Fandom, and their image servers, see your IP address. Animation projects download the anime character model (about 150 MB) once, to compare the pictures. A hero and their everyday self (Ladybug and Marinette) stay two characters.
- **Fast Scan works on anime too,** with the anime models: one 1080p episode took 156 s instead of 416 s on an RTX 3060 Ti. Held against the full scan, it found 96 % of the main cast's heads, and 83 % ended up on the same character.

### Language

- **AutoScene speaks Romanian as well as English.** Screens, menus, dialogs, errors, job titles, the macOS menu and exported names now come from a language catalog. **Settings → General → App language** offers Automatic, English and Română. Automatic follows your system's language list, so a computer set to Romanian starts in it. A choice applies at once, with no restart, though open menus and scroll positions reset. Numbers and plurals follow the language. The Romanian is a first draft, so corrections are very welcome. What Discord shows, the licences in About and a plugin's own words stay as they are. To add a language, start with the [translation guide](https://github.com/tcmmi/AutoScene/blob/main/docs/TRANSLATING.md).

### Analysis and hardware

- **Fast Scan follows your computer.** New projects start with Fast Scan where a full scan is heavy work: built-in graphics, a card without AI hardware (a GTX 1060, say), the processor, all Macs, and Linux, where analysis runs on the processor. Cards built for AI work, such as an RTX card, keep the full scan, and so do graphics AutoScene can't recognise. 3D animation keeps the full scan on Automatic, and so does live action while **Find characters without a visible face** is on. **Settings → General** chooses Automatic, On (which covers 3D animation too) or Off, the New Project dialog has a switch that says what it costs, and a notice at the start says so where it applies, with **Turn off Fast Scan** and **Don't show this again**.
- **Choose what analysis runs on.** **Settings → General → Hardware acceleration** names the device analysis runs on and says when some steps fell back to the processor. On Windows, Automatic now takes the card Windows ranks fastest, not simply the first, which helps laptops with two. You can pick a card when there are two or more, or **Processor only**. On a Mac the row is called **Chip**, with Automatic or Processor only. On Linux, and on Windows with no usable card, it only tells you.
- **NVIDIA acceleration** (Windows x64, GeForce RTX 30 or newer, optional): once you agree to NVIDIA's licence in that row, AutoScene downloads NVIDIA's TensorRT for RTX files (about 111 MB, 286 MB on disk) from the Python Package Index, which sees your IP address, and anime analysis runs on them. Live action and shots don't use it. Whatever goes wrong with those files, analysis goes on without them.
- **Macs keep what Core ML compiled.** A model used to compile again at every start of the engine. It now compiles once per model, shape and runtime, and a compile unused for 30 days is deleted. A kept compile takes about as much disk as its model. How much time this saves on a real Mac is still to be measured.
- **Fewer false shots, and stacks.** A camera move, smoke or a change of light no longer splits a shot like a dissolve: on six test episodes, one-shot spans still split fell from 162 to 20. The price is that 13 of 39 real transitions are now missed (credits, wipes, a lens flare); hard cuts are untouched. In a character's Shots view, shots of one episode that follow each other within 6 seconds, with no fade or black between, stack on one card that scrubs on hover and opens in place. Every shot stays separate, so a scenepack still cuts per shot. Episodes analysed before keep their old shots until they're analysed again.

### Scenepacks and exports

- **A calmer scenepack builder.** **Take every shot**, **Leave every shot out**, **Invert the pack** and **Hand every shot back to the filters** are in the ⋯ beside Export and in the right-click menu, each with Undo. The first three are also on Ctrl+A, Ctrl+Shift+A and Ctrl+Shift+I (⌘ on a Mac); with every shot already in, Ctrl+A leaves them all out. **Hand every shot back** has no key and shows while you have picks by hand. All the filters are in one **Filters** menu with a count, and it stays open for several choices. A plugin's filters show as chips beside it. **Export** now shows its export under way ("Exporting… 12%"), so a pack doesn't get queued twice. A pack exported while episodes are still analysed says that later shots won't be in it.
- **Exports: EDLs, cut lists and easier names.** An export's menu has **Save EDL…** and **Save cut list (CSV)…**, so an editor can rebuild the cut from the episodes. An EDL holds at most 100 frames a second, and neither takes clips of different frame rates. In the scenepack's Export dialog, buttons put Series, Character, Episode, Number, Timecode and Frame where the cursor is (Series and Character when the whole pack is one file), and Ctrl+Z takes them back. Name parts stand out in the field, and a mistyped one turns red there with a suggestion: "Did you mean {episode}?". The finished toast has **Show in folder**, and an export of more than four clips folds them away once it's done.

### Player, cast and keys

- **A player that puts the picture first.** The picture fills the stage, also from a small preview copy that used to stay at 960×540. The details are one panel with **Cast** and **File** tabs that remembers which you left open. In, Out and Export appear once, under the picture, and nothing in that row moves during playback. The timeline sits on the page without its card, drawn at its real size. The key list is a dialog, **Player keys…** in the player menu. A right-click on the timecode picks elapsed, time left, frames or percent. An episode that isn't analysed offers to find its shots and characters right in the Cast tab.
- **A big cast is easier to handle.** From six characters a row finds someone by name, narrows the list and orders it by screen time, shots or name. Ctrl/⌘-click and Shift-click select characters to hide at once, with Undo. **Choose picture…** offers a character's clearest faces. A Maybe face plays its moment where it is, on hover or with Space. A character's picture now comes from their typical faces, so it no longer shows a stray face grouped with them.
- **Your own keys.** **Settings → Shortcuts** lists every shortcut. Click a key and press the new one; a few keys, such as review 1–9, Ctrl/⌘+A and Esc on a selection, and Space and Enter on a card, are shown but can't be changed. Plugin commands have no keys yet. **Keys like in** starts from AutoScene's, Premiere Pro's or DaVinci Resolve's keys. A key another shortcut had is taken from it with an Undo, and Reset all takes everything back.
- **Discord status.** **Settings → Discord** can show that you're working in AutoScene, through the Discord app on your computer. It's off by default, and nothing is sent until you turn it on. Project, episode and character names each have their own switch, also off. The status is always in English, with a **Get AutoScene** button. We haven't tried it against the real Discord app yet, so please tell us what you see.

### Everyday comfort

- **Calmer progress.** A bar that pauses for a moment holds still, with its percent and time left; a longer pause shows the sweep. A bar glides at the pace its updates come and never slides backwards. A waiting job shows an empty, still bar. Under "reduce motion" nothing loops or strobes. The time left shows beside the progress on episodes and in the player, with a Cancel button there too. The indexing screen counts frames and shows its speed. Asking for work already under way no longer starts it over, and an analysis you cancelled stays cancelled after a restart.
- **The pointer tells you what a click does.** Buttons, tabs, switches, an episode row that can play and the timeline's shots show the hand. Disabled controls and menu entries keep the arrow.
- **Toasts show in the installed app.** Until now they sat below the window, so Undo and error toasts never appeared there. The fix was checked on Windows, not yet on a Mac or Linux.
- **Settings.** **Settings → Updates** can turn automatic checks off and links to the release notes. **Settings → Contributors** thanks the people who helped (their pictures come from GitHub, which sees your IP address). moongetsu's issues and pull requests shaped much of this version.
- **New Project.** Files dragged onto the open New Project dialog go into its list, and removing an episode or a project says which of its work stops first. The footage examples in New Project and Edit details now show stills from real films and series.
- **Sturdier.** A hardware check that hangs counts as not available instead of stopping AutoScene, and a slow start of FFmpeg no longer leaves a session unable to export. A clip of one or two frames no longer fails at random on a Mac in Bit-Perfect.
- **The log.** Most log lines no longer name a full path, so the log shows less of your folders. Text quoted from FFmpeg and ffprobe, and a few folder warnings, still can. A file whose path isn't valid Unicode is refused with a reason.

### Installing and updating

- **Going back.** Your projects and models are kept, and their format hasn't changed, so 1.0.3 still opens them if you go back. (It shows a failure saved by this version as raw text, and Fast Scan's default as off.)
- **Two things happen once.** Anime projects you made before start finding their characters as soon as the app opens, after downloading the anime models (about 360 MB): one episode after another, minutes each. Character pictures are picked again in every project; ones you chose stay (**Grouping characters** in the activity list, and **Sharpening portraits** may follow).
- **New projects may start with Fast Scan.** That's new on built-in graphics, older cards, Macs and Linux. Your existing projects keep the scan they have, and if you turned Fast Scan on in 1.0.3, it stays on. **Settings → General** changes it.
- **Not tried on a real Mac yet.** This version was built and tested on GitHub's virtual Macs, which have no Neural Engine or GPU, so a real Mac may behave differently. Still unproven there: the speed and heat of analysis, Fast Scan's default, plugins and `.asp` files, the macOS menu in another language, Discord status, changed keys, and how the player and progress bars look. Linux has run in CI and WSL only, not on a real desktop. If you try either, please tell us what you see.
- **First start on macOS, step by step.** Drag AutoScene to Applications, open it and close the message that macOS can't verify it. Then open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down to the note about AutoScene, choose **Open Anyway** and confirm. (Since macOS 15, right-click → Open no longer does this.) If macOS later refuses a part of the app, such as `libonnxruntime.dylib`, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/AutoScene.app` once.
