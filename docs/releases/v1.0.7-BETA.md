AutoScene 1.0.7 Beta brings AutoScene Cloud: as a closed beta, your videos can now be analysed on a fast cloud graphics card instead of your computer. Scenepacks can take whole scenes, and AutoScene recognises characters better in Fast Scan, anime and 3D animation.

## What's new

- **AutoScene Cloud (closed beta).** No strong graphics card? Turn on **Settings → Cloud → Analyse in the cloud**, and new analyses run on a fast cloud graphics card instead of your computer. Each episode is uploaded, analysed in the cloud and comes back to your project like any other analysis. The file is deleted from the cloud right after. During the beta, scans are free with a beta pass: Beta Testers get one on the AutoScene Discord and redeem it in **Settings → Cloud**.
- **Whole scenes in scenepacks.** Turn on **Read scenes** in **Settings → General** and AutoScene works out which shots belong to one scene. A scenepack's new **Whole scenes** switch then also takes the shots of a character's scenes that they aren't in, such as the other person talking or the reaction. Those shots are marked **Off screen** and come after the character's own.
- **Better Fast Scan.** Fast Scan finds faces more reliably and puts them on the right character more often. In our live-action test, the share of the main cast's screen time that landed on the right character went from 71% to 78%. In our stress test of 27 runs with varied settings, no character was split in two or mixed up with another.
- **Anime characters split in two far less often.** AutoScene often found the same figure twice in one frame, and that split its character in two. That now happens about a third as often, and anime recognition got about a quarter better in our tests, with Standard and with Best.
- **Better episode pictures.** An episode's picture, and so its project's, now shows the moment with the most main characters in view, each clear and big enough. Before, it was a fixed frame that was often a wall, a title or closed eyes. The first minute and a half and the last minute are skipped, so opening titles and end credits are usually not picked.
- **Heroes and their everyday selves stay apart in 3D animation.** In 3D animated series, a masked hero and the same person out of costume often ended up as one character. In series analysed with the full scan, they now usually stay two.
- **Your merges stay merged.** When you merge two characters by hand, they now stay one character after new episodes come in and the cast is grouped again. Moving a single face to another character now moves only that face.
- **Safer plugins.** The part of AutoScene that runs plugins is updated past flaws that let a broken or hostile plugin crash the app or make it use ever more memory.

## Before you install

- **Already installed?** The app updates itself (**Settings → Updates**). Your projects, models, names and decisions are kept.
- **No going back.** 1.0.7 opens all your projects when it starts, and after that 1.0.6 can't open them any more. If you may want to go back, back up your AutoScene library folder first.
- **Some projects catch up once.** Projects with Fast Scan and anime projects find their faces again by themselves, in the background, with the better recognition. On big projects this takes a while. Fast Scan first downloads about 3 MB.
- **3D animation projects group their cast again** if they were analysed with the full scan. Where a hero and their everyday self were one character, they can become two, and the name stays with one of them.
- **AutoScene Cloud is a closed beta.** Scans need a beta pass for now, and adding funds opens after the beta. Each episode is uploaded first, so a fast internet connection helps. Please tell us how it goes.
- **Not tried on a real Mac yet.** We tested on virtual Macs, so speed, heat and some features may differ on yours. Linux hasn't run on a real desktop either, so please tell us what you see.
- **Not signed by a publisher yet,** so the first start needs your OK. On Windows, choose **More info → Run anyway**. On macOS, drag AutoScene to Applications and open it, then choose **Open Anyway** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and confirm. With Smart App Control on, Windows 11 offers no such choice and blocks the installer. If macOS later refuses a part of the app, the fix is in the full list at the very end.

## Check your download

Each file comes with a signed record (a build attestation) of the workflow
of [tcmmi/AutoScene](https://github.com/tcmmi/AutoScene) that built it, and
the tag and commit it was built from. To check one, use the
[GitHub CLI](https://cli.github.com), signed in with any GitHub account:

```bash
gh attestation verify AutoScene_1.0.7-BETA_x64-setup.exe -R tcmmi/AutoScene
```

To insist on this tag and the release workflow, add
`--source-ref refs/tags/v1.0.7-BETA --signer-workflow tcmmi/AutoScene/.github/workflows/release.yml`.

Or compare SHA-256 hashes: put `SHA256SUMS` next to your downloads and run
`sha256sum --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS` (Linux) or
`shasum -a 256 --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS` (macOS). In PowerShell
on Windows, this prints `True` for an intact download:

```powershell
(Get-FileHash .\AutoScene_1.0.7-BETA_x64-setup.exe).Hash -eq (-split (Select-String -Path SHA256SUMS -Pattern x64-setup.exe -SimpleMatch).Line)[0]
```

### AutoScene Cloud (closed beta)

- **Where.** **Settings → Cloud**. The **Analyse in the cloud** switch sends new analyses to the cloud. Turn it off to analyse on your computer again.
- **What happens.** Each episode is uploaded and checked, a cloud graphics card in Europe analyses it, and the results arrive in your project as they come. Activity shows the upload and the start of the cloud graphics card. The uploaded file is deleted from the cloud as soon as the analysis ends. If AutoScene quits during a cloud scan, the file is deleted within 30 minutes.
- **No sign-up.** There is no account to make, no password and no email: AutoScene links the cloud to this computer. Opening **Settings → Cloud** contacts AutoScene Cloud and registers this computer with an anonymous ID. Nothing is uploaded until you turn on **Analyse in the cloud**. **Settings → Cloud** shows a **Support ID** to give us when you need help. It isn't secret.
- **The beta.** Scans need a beta pass, which Beta Testers get on the AutoScene Discord. Redeem it under **Redeem a code**: scans are then free until the date shown. Adding funds opens after the beta.
- **Prices.** **Settings → Cloud** shows what a minute of video costs. Prices can change over time. During the beta, scans stay free with a beta pass.
- **When it can't scan.** The app says why: no beta pass yet, the cloud is busy or paused, the file can't be read as video, and so on.
- **The system's keychain.** The cloud keeps this computer's sign-in in the system's keychain. On a Linux desktop without a working one, the app says it couldn't use the keychain.

### Scenes

- **Read scenes.** A new switch in **Settings → General → Characters**, off by default. With it on, AutoScene also works out which shots belong to one scene. That takes a one-time download of about 90 MB, and each episode takes about a minute longer to analyse on a graphics card. Episodes you analysed before have their scenes read when you turn it on. If reading the scenes of an episode fails or is stopped, the episode says so and lets you continue.
- **Whole scenes.** A switch next to the order on the scenepack page, shown while **Read scenes** is on (or when the pack was saved with it). With it on, the shots of a character's scenes that they aren't in join the pack, marked **Off screen**, after all of the character's own shots. Filters about the character (size, alone, plugin filters) pass these shots by. An off-screen shot stays out, marked **Scene not in pack**, when none of the character's own shots of that scene made it in. Episodes whose scenes aren't read yet keep their shots as before, and the page says how many there are.
- **Best on live action.** Telling scenes apart works best on filmed footage. For anime and animation it's a first version.

### Recognising characters

- **Fast Scan finds faces better.** A new, more accurate face finder. On a hand-checked live-action episode, 78% of the main cast's screen time landed on the right character, against 71% before. In a stress test of 27 runs with varied settings, no character was split or merged, where the old Fast Scan merged characters in 3 runs. It costs a few seconds more per episode on a graphics card.
- **Fast Scan's licence.** Its new face finder may only be used for non-commercial work, like the full scan's models. Settings now says so.
- **Fast Scan wastes less work.** Faces now go to the recognition step in full rounds instead of a few at a time. With Fast Scan on, an anime episode took about a tenth less time on a graphics card in our test.
- **Anime: one figure, one head.** A figure was often counted twice in one frame, once from its head and once from its body, so its character split in two. On our anime test episodes, such doubles fell to about a third. Recognition of the main cast went from 0.34 to 0.43 with Standard and from 0.40 to 0.50 with Best (a score from 0 to 1). On one title, Best now groups a mother with her look-alike daughter a little more often.
- **3D animation: two selves, two characters.** In projects of the **Animation** footage type, two large groups of faces that look too different from each other no longer join into one character. That keeps a hero and their everyday self apart. It works with the full scan, not with Fast Scan, and only once AutoScene has seen enough clear faces, as in a series; a single film or episode usually isn't enough. In our tests on one 3D series, the two selves ended up as one character in 1 of 31 projects, against 7 before.
- **Merges hold.** A merge you make by hand now holds when later episodes bring more faces of the merged-in person, and when the cast is grouped again. A merge made before this version into a character without a name gets this protection once you name that character or merge into it again.
- **A moved face moves alone.** Moving one face to another character could pull that face's whole group along, or join the two characters. Now only the face moves.

### Episode pictures

- **The best moment.** Once an episode's faces are found, its picture becomes the frame with the most main characters, each with a clear, big enough face wholly in the picture, away from cuts. The first minute and a half and the last minute are skipped (never more than a tenth of the episode each), so opening titles and end credits are usually not picked.
- **Kept up to date.** The picture is picked again when the main cast changes: a name, a hidden character, a merge or a new grouping. Pictures from before this version are picked again once after the update, for every episode whose faces are found.

### Other

- **Plugin security fixes.** The runtime for WebAssembly plugins is updated past eight published flaws: a plugin could make AutoScene stop or grow its memory without limit.
- **Romanian.** The new parts are translated as a draft, like the rest of the Romanian words.

### Installing and updating

- **Not tried on a real Mac yet.** This version was built and tested on GitHub's virtual Macs, which have no Neural Engine or GPU, so a real Mac may behave differently. Linux has run in CI and WSL only, not on a real desktop. If you try either, please tell us what you see.
- **First start on macOS, step by step.** Drag AutoScene to Applications, open it and close the message that macOS can't verify it. Then open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll down to the note about AutoScene, choose **Open Anyway** and confirm. (Since macOS 15, right-click → Open no longer does this.) If macOS later refuses a part of the app, such as `libonnxruntime.dylib`, run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/AutoScene.app` once.
